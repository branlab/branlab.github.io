# {% include icon.html icon="fa-solid fa-microscope" %}Research

We use network science to connect minds, behaviour and society. Our work spans four interconnected themes.

{% include section.html %}

## {% include icon.html icon="fa-solid fa-robot" %}Human–AI Interactions

Conversational AI is becoming a social actor. We study how it co-evolves with people, from how one person thinks and feels to how whole social networks are structured. We don't start from the assumption that the effects are good or bad. We ask which patterns emerge, for whom, and under what conditions. The programme runs at five scales:

- **Cognitive–affective dynamics:** how sustained AI use reshapes cognition, emotion and self-perception over time.
- **Asymmetric regulation:** what emotional regulation looks like when one partner has no body or felt state.
- **Relational propagation:** whether AI-linked changes spill over into families, classrooms, workplaces and social networks.
- **Auditing and vulnerability:** which AI behaviour patterns, such as sycophancy, refusal and drift in tone, are harmful, and for whom.
- **Multiscale methods:** tools and generative models for studying minds, bodies and societies as one interdependent system.

{% include section.html %}

## {% include icon.html icon="fa-solid fa-plus-minus" %}Signed Networks

Social ties can be positive or negative: friendship and rivalry, trust and distrust. We develop the theory, measures and dynamics of signed networks to understand polarisation, structural balance and how social systems settle into equilibrium. We also learn the social mechanisms behind these patterns from real temporal data.

{% include section.html %}

## {% include icon.html icon="fa-solid fa-brain" %}Mental Health

The structure of language, cognition and the brain carries signals about mental health. We use cognitive network analysis of real-world narratives, brain and physiological networks, and multimodal digital data to characterise distress, anxiety and mental-health trajectories. We also identify markers that could support earlier and more personalised care.

{% include section.html %}

## {% include icon.html icon="fa-solid fa-virus" %}Epidemiology

We build network and data-driven models to inform public-health decisions. Current work focuses on HIV prevention: reconstructing sexual contact networks and evaluating how PrEP should be distributed, and at what cost, across Europe. We also model outbreak control in care homes and other high-risk settings.
